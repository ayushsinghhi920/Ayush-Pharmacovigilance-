# ADR Identification Basics

## Task 1 — Adverse Drug Reaction Identification

### Objective
Analyze the provided patient scenario, review the medication history and timeline, identify the suspected medicine, and assess whether the reported symptoms could represent an adverse drug reaction (ADR).

---

## 1. Patient Information

| Field | Information |
|---|---|
| Patient | Female |
| Age | 32 years |

## 2. Presenting Symptoms

The patient developed:

- Rash
- Itching
- Nausea
- Dizziness

## 3. Medication History

- **Amoxicillin** — started 5 days before the assessment
- **Paracetamol** — taken as needed

## 4. Timeline

| Time | Event |
|---|---|
| Day 0 | Amoxicillin was started |
| Day 2 | Rash, itching, nausea and dizziness began |
| Day 5 | Symptoms were still present |

The onset of symptoms occurred after amoxicillin was initiated, giving a temporal relationship between exposure and the reported reaction.

---

## 5. ADR Assessment

### Suspected Medicine
**Amoxicillin**

### Suspected Adverse Drug Reaction
**Rash and itching, with nausea and dizziness.**

### Relationship Assessment

The reaction is **suspected to be drug-related**, because the symptoms appeared after amoxicillin was started. The temporal sequence is compatible with a possible adverse drug reaction.

However, the available case information is not sufficient to establish causality with certainty. Other possible causes, including the underlying illness or other exposures, would need to be considered in a real pharmacovigilance assessment.

### Key Reasoning

1. Amoxicillin was started before the symptoms appeared.
2. The symptoms developed approximately two days after initiation.
3. The symptoms persisted while the medication had recently been used.
4. Amoxicillin is therefore the **suspected medicine** in this case.
5. Paracetamol was also reported, but it was taken as needed and the scenario does not provide a comparable temporal relationship.

---

## 6. Conclusion

Based on the supplied patient scenario, **amoxicillin is the suspected drug associated with the reported rash, itching, nausea and dizziness**. The chronology supports a **possible ADR**, but the information provided does not by itself prove causality.

> This is an educational case analysis for the internship task and is not a clinical diagnosis or a substitute for a formal pharmacovigilance causality assessment.
